       what controls the delay, and what forms during it}

        \url{https://github.com/porth-bot/grokking-transformer}}}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
A one-layer decoder-only transformer trained full-batch on 30\% of the
$(a+b) \bmod 97$ table reaches 100\% training accuracy at step 100 and does not
reach 99\% test accuracy for another 1{,}300 steps (median over five seeds).
The phenomenon is \citet{power2022grokking}'s and the Fourier circuit found
inside it is \citet{nanda2023progress}'s; both are reproduced here from a
hand-written implementation, and nothing in this write-up is a new phenomenon.
What it adds is controls and error bars. Weight decay and training fraction
each change the median delay by about a factor of nine across the range swept,
with one end of the weight-decay sweep not grokking at all, and a
$4 \times 3$ grid says they multiply with no interaction detectable at three
seeds per cell --- including the finding that 40\% of the table groks with no
explicit regularizer at all, so ``$wd = 0$ never groks'' was a statement about
the 30\% row. Swapping weight decay for dropout also groks, with the parameter
norm rising rather than falling, and restricting the decay to the embeddings
alone never groks while restricting it to everything else reproduces the full
run. The delay is not a plateau: test accuracy at memorization is
$0.247$ [$0.163$--$0.302$] over five seeds against a chance level of
$1/97 = 0.0103$, and it climbs at nearly every evaluation before the jump.
Thirteen mechanistic read-outs, previously reported from the single run whose
checkpoints the repository commits, are re-measured over five seeds with an
exact rank-sum test: twelve separate memorization from generalization
completely, at the $p = 0.008$ floor the sample size imposes, while the
published effect sizes turn out to be the largest the five seeds contain
because that run was trained five to six times longer than the others.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A network trained on a small algorithmic dataset can fit its training set long
before it learns anything that transfers, and then --- with the training loss
already near zero and nothing obvious left to drive it --- start generalizing.
\citet{power2022grokking} named this grokking and demonstrated it on modular
arithmetic; \citet{nanda2023progress} opened the trained network and found a
Fourier-based angle-addition circuit inside it. This write-up reproduces both
on $(a + b) \bmod 97$ from an implementation written from scratch, and
Figure~\ref{fig:main} is the phenomenon: training accuracy saturates at step
100, test accuracy is still at $0.16$ then, and the run crosses 99\% test
accuracy at step 1{,}900.

Reproducing a known result is worth doing and is not worth writing up on its
own, so it is worth being explicit about what this document adds and what it
does not. It adds nothing about the phenomenon itself. Every mechanism
described here --- the delay, the circuit, the role of weight decay, the
progress measures --- is somebody else's, and Section~\ref{sec:related} says
whose. What it adds is two things that a from-scratch reimplementation is
unusually well placed to supply, because they cost training runs rather than
ideas.

The first is \textbf{controls}: arms whose job is to rule an explanation out.
Sweeping weight decay against training fraction on a grid rather than along two
axes (Section~\ref{sec:surface}) turns ``$wd = 0$ never groks'' from a property
of the method into a property of one row, since 40\% of the table groks in
every seed with no explicit regularizer. Swapping weight decay for dropout
(Section~\ref{sec:controls-arms}) separates ``norm pressure'' from ``pressure
against memorizing'': dropout groks too, and its parameter norm rises
throughout. Restricting which parameters the decay touches
(Section~\ref{sec:scope}) locates the effect on the readout circuit rather than
on the embeddings, where the Fourier structure lives. A ten-fold learning-rate
range and a change of modulus check that the phenomenon is not an artifact of
one schedule or one $p$.

The second is \textbf{error bars on mechanistic read-outs}, which are usually
reported from a single run because they are read off committed checkpoints and
a checkpoint is one run by construction. Section~\ref{sec:seeds} re-measures
all thirteen of this repository's read-outs across five seeds and tests each
with an exact rank-sum permutation test. The qualitative story survives
everywhere it is checked; the published numbers do not, and they fail in one
direction, for a reason (Section~\ref{sec:seeds}) that is worth more than the
corrected numbers themselves.

Concretely, this write-up contributes:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A reproduction of grokking on modular addition in a hand-written
    one-layer transformer, with attention and LayerNorm implemented rather
    than imported and checked against PyTorch's reference implementations
    (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
  \item A $4 \times 3$ weight-decay $\times$ data-fraction grid with
    right-censoring treated as censoring rather than dropped or imputed to the
    budget, showing that data substitutes for weight decay multiplicatively
    and that no interaction is detectable at this resolution
    (Section~\ref{sec:surface}).
  \item Four controls --- learning rate, regularizer, modulus, and the
    \emph{scope} of the weight decay --- of which the last two report results
    that qualify accounts in the literature rather than confirming them
    (Sections~\ref{sec:controls-arms} and~\ref{sec:scope}).
  \item Five-seed measurements, with an exact permutation test and its floor
    reported alongside every $p$-value, for thirteen mechanistic read-outs
    that this repository previously published from one run
    (Section~\ref{sec:seeds}).
  \item Scope checks over head count and over three binary operations,
    including the observation that the operand-swap symmetry a grokked model
    acquires is the one its operation actually has, which for $a - b$ is
    anti-equivariance and not commutativity (Sections~\ref{sec:heads}
    and~\ref{sec:ops}).
\end{itemize}

Several claims that this repository had previously published did not survive
being measured again, and they are reported in place as failures rather than
quietly corrected: a mechanistic explanation of the head-count ordering
(Section~\ref{sec:heads}), a story about multiplication grokking sooner
(Section~\ref{sec:ops}), an embedding/logit frequency overlap presented as
evidence about the transition (Section~\ref{sec:attribution}), and a claim that
the transition is softer at a larger modulus (Section~\ref{sec:controls-arms}).

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model.} A one-layer decoder-only transformer
\citep{vaswani2017attention} with pre-LayerNorm residual blocks:
$d_{\text{model}} = 128$, 4 heads of width 32, an MLP of width 512, learned
positional embeddings, and a vocabulary of 98 (the 97 residues plus an ``=''
token), for 223{,}360 parameters. The attention arithmetic and the LayerNorm
are written out rather than called from a fused library op, and both are
checked against PyTorch's reference implementations in the test suite ---
which is the point of a from-scratch reimplementation and also the only reason
to trust the read-outs of Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}, since they index into
internals that a fused kernel does not expose. Dropout is $0.0$, and therefore
an exact identity, in every run here except the regularizer control of
Section~\ref{sec:controls-arms}.

\paragraph{Data.} All $p^2 = 9{,}409$ ordered pairs for $p = 97$, encoded as
the length-3 sequence $[a, b, =]$ with the answer supervised at the ``=''
position. Train and test are a random partition of that finite universe: at
30\% it is 2{,}823 training pairs against 6{,}586 test. The task is exhaustive
and noiseless, so ``test accuracy'' means recovering the map rather than
smoothing over noise, memorization achieves exactly the training set, and
chance is $1/97 = 0.0103$ --- a level worth stating because two of this paper's
corrections turn on how far above it the delay actually sits.

\paragraph{Optimization.} Full-batch AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}, learning
rate $10^{-3}$, $\beta = (0.9, 0.98)$. Full batch because the dataset fits in
one tensor and minibatch noise would otherwise be an uncontrolled variable in
every sweep below. Decoupled decay rather than L2 folded into the loss because
those are different regularizers once Adam's preconditioner is applied, and
weight decay is this paper's main independent variable. Both splits are
evaluated every 100 steps, which is the resolution of every step count quoted
here.

\paragraph{Definitions.} The \emph{memorization step} is the first evaluation
at which training accuracy reaches 99.9\%; on the main configuration it is step
100 in every seed of every arm, and training accuracy there is exactly $1.0$.
The \emph{grok step} is the first evaluation at which test accuracy reaches
99\%. A run stops early once test accuracy holds $\ge 99.9\%$ for a set number
of consecutive evaluations, and otherwise at a budget of 25{,}000 steps
(15{,}000 for the decay-scope arm of Section~\ref{sec:scope}). A run that never
reaches the grok threshold inside its budget has not produced a large grok
step; it has produced none, bounded below by the budget, and it is treated as
right-censored throughout --- neither dropped nor imputed
(Section~\ref{sec:surface}).

\paragraph{Seeds.} A seed sets both the train/test partition and the
initialization, so a seed spread here bundles those two sources of variation
and cannot separate them. How many seeds a claim rests on varies by experiment
and is stated with each: five for the weight-decay and data-fraction sweeps
(Sections~\ref{sec:wd} and~\ref{sec:frac}), the head-count arms
(Section~\ref{sec:heads}) and the mechanistic read-outs
(Section~\ref{sec:seeds}); three for the $wd \times \mathrm{frac}$ grid
(Section~\ref{sec:surface}), the operations comparison and the operand-swap
read-outs (Section~\ref{sec:ops}); and one for the learning-rate, dropout,
modulus and decay-scope controls, for the 60\% fraction point, and for the two
instrumented trajectories of Sections~\ref{sec:progress}
and~\ref{sec:attention}. The single-seed arms are stated as directions rather
than as effect sizes wherever they appear. What each sample size can and cannot
certify is arithmetic rather than judgement, and
Sections~\ref{sec:surface} and~\ref{sec:seeds} give it: the exact test used
throughout has a two-sided floor of $2/\binom{10}{5} = 0.008$ at five versus
five and $2/\binom{6}{3} = 0.10$ at three versus three, so the grid ranks cells
and certifies no pairwise comparison.

One asymmetry among the five main-configuration seeds matters enough to state
here rather than only where it bites. Seed~0 is the run whose checkpoints the
repository commits, and it was configured to keep training for 30 evaluations
past the threshold rather than 5, so it ran 11{,}100 steps where seeds 1--4
stop at 1{,}800--2{,}100. Every trajectory that continues past the transition
is therefore seed~0's by necessity, and every mechanistic read-out taken at a
``final'' checkpoint is comparing one long run against four short ones.
Section~\ref{sec:seeds} measures what that does.

\section{What controls the delay}
\label{sec:controls}

\subsection{Weight decay}
\label{sec:wd}

The first question is whether the delay is a property of the task or of the
regularizer. Table~\ref{tab:wd} sweeps weight decay at 30\% training data,
five seeds per arm, holding everything else at the main configuration.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Weight decay at 30\% training data, five seeds per arm. Every seed
  of every arm memorizes at step 100; what changes is whether and when test
  accuracy reaches 99\%.}
  \label{tab:wd}
  \begin{tabular}{lccc}
    \toprule
    weight decay & memorized & grokked, median [min--max] & final test acc. \\
    \midrule
    $0.0$ & 100 & never (25k budget, 5/5 seeds) & 0.29--0.42 \\
    $0.1$ & 100 & 10{,}800 [7{,}600--13{,}900]  & $\ge 0.999$ \\
    $1.0$ & 100 & 1{,}300 [1{,}200--1{,}900]    & $1.000$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three things follow, and only the first is about the size of the effect. The
arms are separated by far more than their own spread: the slowest $wd = 1$ seed
groks at 1{,}900 and the fastest $wd = 0.1$ seed at 7{,}600, so no relabelling
of seeds within the sweep could reorder the arms. The unregularized control
memorizes exactly as fast and then stays memorized, ending at 0.29--0.42 test
accuracy --- some implicit regularization, no transition. And after step
${\sim}100$ the training loss is already near zero, so almost everything that
happens to test accuracy afterwards happens under the norm-pressure term rather
than under the task gradient.

The scope of the middle claim needs stating carefully, because this sweep
cannot establish it. ``$wd = 0$ never groks'' is measured \emph{at 30\% data}.
Section~\ref{sec:surface} runs the same control at 40\% and it groks in all
three seeds, so the sentence is a statement about that row and not about the
method. A one-variable-at-a-time design cannot tell those apart, which is the
reason the grid in Section~\ref{sec:surface} exists.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{wd_sweep.pdf}
  \caption{Test accuracy against step at three weight decays, 30\% training
  data. Line is the median over five seeds, band the interquartile range; the
  legend gives each arm's grok step as median [min--max] over those seeds. The
  $wd = 0$ arm never reaches the threshold inside the budget and is reported as
  such rather than extrapolated.}

\subsection{Data fraction}
\label{sec:frac}

The complementary sweep holds $wd = 1$ and varies how much of the
$97 \times 97$ table the network is trained on (Table~\ref{tab:frac}). The
delay grows as the data shrinks, monotonically in the medians and roughly
log-linearly.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Training fraction at $wd = 1$, five seeds per cell except the 60\%
  context point, which is one seed and carries no spread for that reason.}
  \label{tab:frac}
  \begin{tabular}{lcc}
    \toprule
    train fraction & grok step, median [min--max] & delay over memorization \\
    \midrule
    25\% & 2{,}700 [2{,}000--3{,}100] & $27\times$ \\
    30\% & 1{,}300 [1{,}200--1{,}900] & $13\times$ \\
    40\% & 300 [300--700]             & $3\times$  \\
    60\% & 200 (1 seed)               & $2\times$  \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The mechanism the numbers are consistent with: as the training set shrinks,
memorizing it gets cheaper (there are fewer pairs to store) while the general
circuit's cost is fixed, so the phase in which memorization is the better deal
stretches. At 60\% the delay has essentially vanished and what is left is
ordinary learning --- grokking here is a small-data phenomenon. Whether the two
knobs of this section trade off is the next subsection's question, and it is
not answerable from these two slices.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{frac_sweep.pdf}

  trajectories at 25/30/40\% training data, median over five seeds with an

  fraction, median with min--max whiskers. The 60\% point is a single-seed

\subsection{Do they trade off? The $wd \times \mathrm{frac}$ surface}
\label{sec:surface}

Both preceding sweeps are monotone, and both are consistent with a story
neither can test: that the two knobs trade off, so enough data would buy
grokking at a weight decay that cannot produce it alone. Reading a trade-off
off two orthogonal slices is exactly the inference a one-variable-at-a-time
design invites and cannot support. Table~\ref{tab:surface} is the
$4 \times 3$ grid that tests it, three seeds per cell.

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Median grok step over three seeds, [min--max]. ``$> 25{,}000$''
  marks a cell whose median is right-censored at the budget.}
  \label{tab:surface}
  \small
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    train frac & $wd = 0$ & $wd = 0.1$ & $wd = 0.3$ & $wd = 1$ \\
    \midrule
    25\% & $> 25{,}000$ & $> 25{,}000$          & 10{,}700 [8{,}300--12{,}400] & 3{,}100 [2{,}700--3{,}100] \\
    30\% & $> 25{,}000$ & 10{,}800 [9{,}200--13{,}900] & 4{,}900 [4{,}100--5{,}300] & 1{,}300 [1{,}200--1{,}900] \\
    40\% & 2{,}100 [600--9{,}900] & 1{,}400 [500--3{,}400] & 700 [400--1{,}800] & 500 [300--700] \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The censoring convention.} A cell whose seeds never generalize
inside the budget has not produced a large number; it has produced \emph{no}
number, bounded below by 25{,}000. Both tempting alternatives are silently
wrong: dropping such cells biases the surface toward the fast corner, and
imputing the budget invents a measurement. What is done instead is the standard
right-censored treatment. A cell's seeds are sorted with the censored ones at
$+\infty$, which leaves the median \emph{exact} whenever fewer than half of
them are censored and reports a lower bound otherwise; the heatmap hatches
those cells rather than colouring them, so no gradient can be read across a
region where no gradient was measured. Two of the three censored cells here
have 3/3 seeds censored; the $(25\%, wd\,{=}\,0.1)$ cell has 2/3, with its
one open seed at 23{,}100, which is enough to censor the median and not enough
to identify it.

\paragraph{Data substitutes for weight decay.} Down the $wd = 0$ column, 25\%
and 30\% never grok --- 3 of 3 seeds each, at the full budget --- and 40\%
groks in all three, median 2{,}100, ending at test accuracy 0.9993, 0.9996 and
0.9998. So Section~\ref{sec:wd}'s control is not a fact about $wd = 0$: with
40\% of the addition table the network gets there with no explicit regularizer
at all. This is a claim about which cells produced a number and which produced
none, so no seed spread and no fit can move it.

\paragraph{But trading off is not interacting, and the interaction is not
there.} In logs the no-interaction null is additive,
$\log_{10} T = \mu + a_{\text{frac}} + b_{wd}$ --- changing $wd$ multiplies
the delay by the same factor at every fraction. Fitted over the nine cells with
an identified median (3 residual degrees of freedom) it holds well: rms
residual $1.24\times$, worst cell $1.51\times$, against within-cell seed
spreads of $1.15\times$ to $16.50\times$. The fitted effects are two
independent multipliers, $2.81 / 1.36 / 0.26$ for the fractions and
$2.09 / 1.69 / 0.86 / 0.33$ for the weight decays.

The censored cells look like they refute that, and the check that kills the
refutation is worth stating because the refutation is the more interesting
claim. A censored cell carries a real inequality the least-squares fit cannot
use: the null predicts 22{,}565 / 18{,}327 / 10{,}915 steps for
$(25\%, 0)$, $(25\%, 0.1)$ and $(30\%, 0)$, and all three medians are bounded
below by 25{,}000 --- bound ratios of $1.11\times$, $1.36\times$ and
$2.29\times$, the last apparently well outside the fit's own $1.51\times$
worst case. But the $wd = 0$ column effect rests on the \emph{single} open
cell in that column, whose three seeds span $16.5\times$. Refitting at the
corners of the open cells' observed seed ranges gives envelopes of
$[0.11, 9.95]$, $[0.51, 4.65]$ and $[0.19, 18.99]$, every one of which
straddles~1. The censoring is consistent with pure multiplicative
independence: the $wd = 0$ column is simply slow enough that 25\% and 30\%
fall past the budget and 40\% does not. There is no cliff here, only the edge
of the compute.

So the summary is the weaker and more useful one: data substitutes for weight
decay, multiplicatively, with no synergy detectable at this resolution. Three
seeds per cell is also worth reading as a limit rather than a sample --- the
exact test of Section~\ref{sec:seeds} has a two-sided floor of
$2/\binom{6}{3} = 0.10$ at three versus three, so this grid ranks cells and
compares steps against spreads but certifies no pairwise comparison at the
level Section~\ref{sec:heads} reaches. The 40\% row in particular
\emph{looks} flatter than the rows above ($1.4\times$ across
$wd\,{=}\,0.3 \to 1$ against $3.45\times$ and $3.77\times$), which would be
an interaction, but its seed spreads there are $2.33\times$ and $4.50\times$,
so the flattening is inside the noise and is not claimed.

\subsection{Controls: learning rate, regularizer, modulus}
\label{sec:controls-arms}

Three arms whose job is to rule things out rather than to show something. Each
is one seed per cell, so they are stated as directions and not as effect sizes.

\paragraph{Learning rate.} Rerunning the main configuration across a
$10\times$ range of learning rates leaves the phenomenon intact and moves the
grok step roughly inversely: 5{,}500 / 1{,}700 / 800 steps at
$3\times10^{-4}$ / $10^{-3}$ / $3\times10^{-3}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lr}).
Memorization is already near-instant at all three, so the delay
\emph{multiple} shrinks as the learning rate grows without vanishing. The
consequence for everything else here is that grok steps are comparable only at
fixed learning rate --- all other sweeps hold $10^{-3}$ --- and that
``1{,}300 steps'' is a property of the optimizer schedule as much as of the
task.

\paragraph{Regularizer.} Section~\ref{sec:wd} shows weight decay driving the
transition, and the norm-based account of \citet{liu2023omnigrok} makes norm
reduction the mechanism. That invites the control of swapping weight decay for
dropout, which does not target the norm at all. Dropout $0.1$ at $wd = 0$
groks, at step 3{,}500, to final test accuracy $0.999$
(Figure~\ref{fig:dropout}). So it is not weight decay specifically that is
required --- but the route is visibly different: under dropout the parameter
norm \emph{rises} monotonically throughout, $21 \to 55$, while the $wd = 1$
run generalizes with the norm coming down. Norm reduction is sufficient here
and not necessary. What the two share, and what the unregularized run lacks, is
pressure against the pure-memorization solution rather than any particular way
of applying it.

\paragraph{Modulus.} Repeating the main configuration at $p = 113$ changes two
things at once: more residue classes for the circuit to represent, and --- at a
fixed 30\% fraction --- more absolute training pairs, 3{,}831 against 2{,}823.
The larger modulus groks \emph{sooner}, at step 600 against 1{,}900, so
time-to-grok is not a clean increasing function of $p$ and the data-quantity
effect wins on this axis. This is the same lever as Section~\ref{sec:frac}.
One thing this arm does \emph{not} show, though an earlier version of it
claimed to: that the transition is ``softer'' at the larger modulus. Its test
accuracy at memorization is $0.288$, and the five $p = 97$ seeds span
$0.163$--$0.302$ on that statistic, so the $p = 113$ run sits inside the range
of the modulus it was being contrasted with. What looked like a difference was
one seed against one seed, and the $p = 97$ seed chosen was the lowest of the
five.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{lr_sweep.pdf}

\subsection{Where the norm pressure has to act}
\label{sec:scope}

``The weight norm'' is the norm of every parameter, so the previous subsection
leaves a question it cannot answer: does the decay need to act on the
\emph{embeddings}, where the Fourier structure of Section~\ref{sec:fourier}
lives, or on the rest of the network, which reads that structure out? This
ablation holds the main configuration fixed and changes only which parameters
the decay is applied to; the untargeted group trains at $wd = 0$.

The answer is unambiguous, and it is the readout circuit
(Figure~\ref{fig:scope}). Decaying the non-embedding parameters alone
reproduces the full-decay run almost exactly --- grok step 1{,}800 against
1{,}900, with the two accuracy curves overlapping. Decaying only the
embeddings does essentially nothing: the model never groks in 15{,}000 steps
and ends at $0.36$ test accuracy, while the rest of the network, now
unconstrained, keeps its large memorization weights and the total norm climbs
from $21$ to $287$ against the $20$--$40$ the two grokking runs hold. So the
embeddings supply the Fourier basis but their \emph{scale} is not what
memorization exploits, and pinning it is neither sufficient nor the operative
lever. Read against \citet{liu2023omnigrok}, this locates the norm effect
rather than contradicting it: the quantity that matters is not
$\lVert\theta\rVert$ over the whole network.

\section{What forms during the delay}
\label{sec:mechanism}

\subsection{Norm and Fourier structure}
\label{sec:fourier}

Two measurements on the main run, one along the trajectory and one between its
two committed checkpoints.

The parameter norm rises while the task gradient dominates, peaks around the
transition, and falls afterwards once the training loss is pinned near zero and
the decay is the only force left (Figure~\ref{fig:main}, right). It is
tempting to read that shape as the signature of grokking, and it is not:
Section~\ref{sec:surface}'s $wd = 0$ runs at 40\% data reach
${\sim}0.999$ test accuracy with a norm that never once falls below its running
peak --- maximum drawdown $0.00\%$ across every eval of all three seeds,
ending at $722$, $76$ and $32$ from an initial $21.7$ --- while the $wd = 1$
runs fall 7--23\% from their peak. The rise-then-fall belongs to the
weight-decay-driven transition, not to the transition as such. A network can
grok with a monotonically growing norm.

The embedding spectrum is the algorithm's fingerprint and it moves the way the
circuit story requires (Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum}). At memorization the
spectral energy is spread across all 48 frequencies; afterwards a handful
dominate. On the published run the top-5 share goes $13.6\% \to 56.7\%$, but
that pair is the largest contrast the five seeds contain and
Section~\ref{sec:seeds} explains why; the five-seed figures are
$15.4\%$ [$13.6$--$16.5$] $\to 41.4\%$ [$29.2$--$56.7$]. \emph{Which}
frequencies is seed-specific --- every seed picks a different handful --- so
what is portable is the count: reaching 90\% of the embedding energy takes
$42.2$ [$42$--$43$] of the 48 frequencies at memorization and $20.0$
[$11$--$28$] afterwards, in every seed. The geometric face of the same
sparsification is Figure~\ref{fig:circle}, where the embeddings projected on
the dominant frequency's plane tighten onto a ring (radial coefficient of
variation $0.343 \to 0.130$, complete separation across seeds).

One statistic in that figure is a noise floor rather than a measurement, and it
takes a baseline to see it. Unstructured Gaussian embeddings read a radial CV
of $0.428$ and put $4.0\%$ of their variance in the best of the 48 planes ---
$4.0\%$ rather than the naive $2/d_{\text{model}} = 1.6\%$ because choosing
the best of 48 inflates it. The memorization checkpoint reads $4.3\%$, i.e.\
exactly that floor, so only the radial CV carries anything there.

\subsection{The generalizing solution is a sparse sum over frequencies}
\label{sec:attribution}

The previous subsection reads the weights. This one reads the model's output.
If the network has learned the algorithm its logits should depend on $a + b$
and be built from few frequencies --- the angle-addition circuit
$\text{logit}(a,b,c) \approx \sum_k A_k \cos\!\big(\tfrac{2\pi k}{p}
(a + b - c)\big)$. Both are directly measurable: 2D-Fourier-transform the
logit tensor $L[a,b,c]$ over the two input axes, where a function of $a+b$ puts
all its energy on the diagonal $k_a = k_b$.

Over five seeds, the share of logit energy on that diagonal goes
$0.192$ [$0.123$--$0.240$] at memorization to $0.975$ [$0.949$--$0.984$] after
grokking, with complete separation. The reference those percentages need is
that unstructured logits put $2 \cdot 48 / (p^2 - 1) = 1.0\%$ of their energy
on the diagonal, not zero --- so memorization's $19\%$ is already
$19\times$ chance, which is the next paragraph's point, and the grokked model
is near the ceiling. Keeping only the top-$m$ diagonal frequencies and
inverse-transforming rebuilds the logits from just those frequencies, a static
version of \citet{nanda2023progress}'s restricted loss; three frequencies
recover $0.971$ [$0.928$--$1.000$] of test accuracy and it takes 3 to 5 (mean
$3.8$) to clear $0.99$. ``A handful'' is the portable statement; ``three'' is
seed 0's.

The number that matters most for the argument is what the same restriction does
to the \emph{memorizing} checkpoint. Projecting its logits onto the clean
$a+b$ subspace recovers far more test accuracy than the raw memorizing model
expresses: $0.817$ [$0.774$--$0.880$] with ten frequencies kept, against the
model's own $0.247$ [$0.163$--$0.302$], with every seed's projection above
every seed's raw accuracy. The generalizing circuit is already present,
drowned out by per-pair memorization, before the test accuracy moves. This is
one of the few claims here that got \emph{stronger} when it was averaged.

One reading offered by an earlier version of this analysis does not survive.
The dominant logit frequencies do overlap the dominant embedding frequencies
well above chance ($3.0$ of 5 against $0.52$ expected), which was presented as
evidence that the logits are written in the basis the embeddings carry. But the
same overlap is $3.4$ of 5 \emph{at memorization}: it does not separate the
checkpoints ($p = 0.76$) and so cannot be evidence about the transition. It is
a fact about the architecture.

\subsection{Progress measures}
\label{sec:progress}

Sections~\ref{sec:fourier} and~\ref{sec:attribution} read two checkpoints.
This one makes the same quantities a trajectory, by rerunning the main
configuration with per-eval instrumentation. The key $a+b$ frequencies are read
off the \emph{final} model and held fixed along the trajectory, so what is
tracked is one circuit forming rather than a moving target, and both loss
measures are computed over all $p^2$ pairs, which decouples them from the
train/test split. Following \citet{nanda2023progress}: the \emph{restricted}
loss keeps only those five frequencies of $a+b$, the \emph{excluded} loss
removes exactly those five and keeps everything else.

The circuit is legible in the loss before it is legible in the accuracy. At the
memorization point the full model's test loss is $5.03$ while the restricted
loss is $4.10$ --- projecting onto five frequencies is already ${\sim}0.9$
nats better than the whole memorizing model. That is the reproduction of
\citet{nanda2023progress}'s central observation, and it holds here through
step 1{,}000.

It does not hold all the way to the jump, and the difference is worth stating
because the tidier version of this sentence is the one that suggests itself.
Both losses fall through the delay, and the full model overtakes the restricted
one at step 1{,}100 --- $3.29$ against $3.31$ --- some 500 steps before the
accuracy jump at 1{,}600. Relatedly, the test accuracy underneath all of this
is not flat: it climbs from $0.163$ at memorization to $0.527$ at step
1{,}500, rising at every eval but one, and then jumps to $0.998$ by 1{,}900.
What is sudden is the crossing of the 99\% threshold that \emph{defines} the
grok step, not the model's improvement. That is a difference of setup rather
than of substance from \citet{nanda2023progress} --- a different modulus,
architecture size and optimizer will put the accuracy plateau at a different
height --- but it is the reason this paper describes a delay rather than a
plateau.

The other two read-outs behave as the picture predicts. Embedding structure
rises gradually, from $14\%$ of the spectral energy in its top five
frequencies at memorization to ${\sim}47\%$ by the end of the instrumented
run. It does begin to climb before the accuracy step, but ``begins to climb''
is true of almost any rising quantity and is not the useful measurement:
counted from its memorization value to the run's maximum, and generously (its
highest pre-jump reading, $0.181$ at step 900, against $0.136$ and $0.477$),
only $13\%$ of the rise precedes the accuracy jump, with the rest arriving at
and after it. And the model genuinely depends on those frequencies: after
grokking the full test loss reaches ${\sim}10^{-2}$ while the excluded loss
holds a median of $0.97$, so removing five of 48 frequencies collapses the
solution.

\subsection{Attention}
\label{sec:attention}

The ``='' position, where the answer is written, spends nearly all its
attention on the two operand positions at both checkpoints. What grokking
changes is the \emph{symmetry} of that read: the per-head asymmetry
$\lvert A_{=\to a} - A_{=\to b}\rvert$ falls $0.119 \to 0.0040$ over five
seeds with complete separation, matching the commutativity the general
algorithm has to respect (Figures~\ref{fig:attnpattern}
and~\ref{fig:attnentropy}).

Two features of the trajectory are worth more than the endpoints. A randomly
initialized model is \emph{already} at the algorithmic symmetry --- operand
entropy $\ln 2$ exactly, since a near-uniform softmax is commutative for free.
Memorization destroys it, driving the per-head asymmetry from $0.004$ at
initialization to $0.189$ at the memorization checkpoint --- both on the
instrumented run, which is why the second is seed 0's value rather than the
$0.119$ five-seed mean above --- and grokking puts it back. The model does not learn commutativity so
much as recover it. And the read-out is a \emph{lagging} indicator: the
symmetry is restored at step 2{,}300, after test accuracy passes $0.5$ at
1{,}500 and after the grok step at 1{,}900. Unlike the restricted loss of
Section~\ref{sec:progress} it is no early warning.

Read through the usual ``attention sharpens'' lens the entropy trajectory is
misleading twice over. Entropy \emph{rises} through grokking, $0.677 \to
0.935$, partly because an even operand split is correct rather than sloppy
under commutativity, and partly because a self-attention channel opens after
the jump. That channel carries no information --- position 2 is the ``='' token
in every example, so the value vector the ``='' query pulls from itself has an
across-batch standard deviation of exactly zero. It is a learned bias, and it
is what the operand-renormalized entropy divides out. It is also why the
operand-weight statistic is the one read-out here that does not separate the
checkpoints cleanly (Section~\ref{sec:seeds}).

Beyond that, these read-outs earn their place in this paper as a null rather
than as a finding: Section~\ref{sec:heads} shows every head-count arm reaching
the same values.

\subsection{Are the mechanistic numbers typical?}
\label{sec:seeds}

Everything above that reads structure out of weights was, until this section,
measured on one run's two checkpoints, because seed 0 is the run whose weights
the repository commits. That is thirteen read-outs from one pair of files.
Here they are all re-measured across the five main-configuration seeds and
tested with an exact two-sample rank-sum permutation test, enumerating all
$\binom{10}{5} = 252$ relabelings rather than approximating.

\paragraph{The qualitative story survives everywhere.} Twelve of the thirteen
read-outs separate memorization from generalization \emph{completely} --- no
memorization checkpoint on the wrong side of any final one --- which at five
seeds per arm produces $p = 0.008$. That is the floor: the smallest two-sided
$p$ the test can return at this sample size is $2/\binom{10}{5} = 0.0079$, so
complete separation is exactly what it takes to reach it and nothing at five
versus five can do better. The test reports that floor beside the $p$-value
precisely so ``not significant'' can be distinguished from ``cannot be
significant at this sample size'', a distinction five seeds per arm forces
constantly. The one read-out that does not separate cleanly is the ``=''
token's operand weight ($p = 0.032$): two grokked runs keep more operand weight
than the least concentrated memorizing one, and one seed moves it the wrong way
entirely ($0.960 \to 0.966$). The self-attention channel of
Section~\ref{sec:attention} predicts exactly that instability, so the finding
is that the \emph{size} of that fall was never measurable from one run.

\paragraph{The published numbers do not survive, and they fail in one
direction.} Seed 0 holds the extreme in the flattering direction on 8 of the 13
read-outs and lies outside the other four's entire range on 7, so nearly every
contrast quoted from it is the largest one the five seeds contain
(Table~\ref{tab:seed0}).

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Contrasts as the published single run reported them, and as five
  seeds report them.}
  \label{tab:seed0}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    from seed 0 alone & over five seeds \\
    \midrule
    $12\% \to 98\%$ on the $a{+}b$ diagonal & $19\% \to 97.5\%$ \\
    equivariance defect $0.189 \to 0.00017$ ($1100\times$) & $0.119 \to 0.0042$ ($28\times$) \\
    top-5 embedding share $13.6\% \to 56.7\%$ & $15.4\% \to 41.4\%$ \\
    three frequencies rebuild 100\% & 3--5 frequencies; three give $0.97$ \\
    operand weight $99.7\% \to 83.7\%$ & $98.1\% \to 90.9\%$, ranges overlapping \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{It is not a lucky seed, and that is the more useful answer.} The
five runs are not the same length. Seed 0 was deliberately extended to 11{,}100
steps --- Section~\ref{sec:fourier}'s norm decline is only visible in a run
that continues past the jump --- while the other four early-stop on patience at
1{,}800--2{,}100. So its final checkpoint has ${\sim}9{,}000$ more steps of
weight decay after grokking than any other, and the same repository's
trajectory shows the circuit continuing to sparsify over exactly that interval.
The seven read-outs on which seed 0 is an outlier are the seven that further
consolidation would move: the sparsity of the embedding spectrum, the ring's
variance share, and all three symmetry defects. With one long run there is no
way to separate ``lucky seed'' from ``trained longer'', so the honest move is
to drop it: rerunning every test on seeds 1--4 alone, where four per arm still
permits complete separation at a floor of $2/\binom{8}{4} = 0.029$, leaves 12
of 13 separating completely, with the same exception now at $p = 0.114$. The
confound reaches the effect sizes and none of the conclusions. What this study
cannot say is how much of ``grokking sparsifies the circuit'' is grokking and
how much is the 9{,}000 steps afterwards; that needs the four short runs
retrained to a matched budget, which has not been done.

The general lesson is worth separating from this repository's version of it.
Section~\ref{sec:heads} finds the same seed 0 to be the \emph{slowest} run in
all three head-count arms, so the single-run timing numbers were uniformly
pessimistic while the single-run mechanistic numbers are uniformly flattering.
Those are not the same phenomenon --- the timing spread is seed noise, the
mechanistic gap traces to a run length chosen by hand --- but a single-run
table cannot tell you which kind of run it got, in either direction.

\subsection{Head count}
\label{sec:heads}

Modular addition has a single known mechanism --- embed each input on a circle,
add the angles, read off the sum by interference --- and nothing in it
obviously needs the representation split across heads. Varying only
\texttt{n\_heads} with $d_{\text{model}}$ pinned at 128, so head width tracks
the count and the parameter count is identical (223{,}360) in all three arms,
five seeds each:

  \centering

  three arms grok to 100\% in every seed.}
  \label{tab:heads}
  \begin{tabular}{lcccc}
    \toprule
    \texttt{n\_heads} & $d_{\text{head}}$ & grok step, median [min--max] & seed spread & final test \\
    \midrule
    1 & 128 & 300 [300--400]           & $1.3\times$ & $1.000$ \\
    2 & 64  & 700 [700--900]           & $1.3\times$ & $1.000$ \\

So grokking on this task does not require multiple heads, and fewer heads grok
sooner. The ordering separates completely --- no seed of a smaller-head arm is
slower than any seed of a larger one, in all three pairwise comparisons, so
every pair sits at $p = 0.008$, the floor of Section~\ref{sec:seeds}'s test.
Why it separates is readable off the table without any test, and that is the
more durable form of the statement: the within-arm seed spread
($1.3$--$1.6\times$) is smaller than the step between arms ($2.33\times$ and
$1.86\times$ on medians). An ordering is resolvable exactly when that
inequality holds.

This section is also the clearest case in this study of a single-seed table
being right about the comparison and wrong about the level. It previously
reported seed 0 alone, at 400 / 900 / 1{,}900, which are the \emph{maximum} of
each arm --- seed 0 is the slowest seed at every head count --- so the shipped
numbers were uniformly pessimistic while the ratios between them
($2.25\times$, $2.11\times$) survived averaging nearly unchanged
($2.33\times$, $1.86\times$).

\paragraph{The mechanistic explanation that came with it did not survive.}
The ordering used to be explained by saying that a one-head circuit lives in a
single attention pattern while more heads must coordinate the computation
across a partitioned residual stream. Measuring
Section~\ref{sec:attention}'s four attention read-outs on all 15 final
checkpoints says the arms end up in the same place: every arm reaches operand
entropy $\ln 2 = 0.6931$ to four decimals, and \emph{none} of the four
read-outs separates any pair of arms under the test that separates every pair
on grok step (smallest $p$ over the 12 comparisons: $0.056$). So head count
changes how long the circuit takes to form, not what it converges to, and
whatever slows the four-head runs is not visible in the endpoint attention.
Running the test on the null claim is what makes that statable: ``the arms end
up the same'' is worth asserting only with the same instrument that established
they differ. One caveat about the statistic rather than the result: it is a
mean over heads, so the four-head number averages four values where the
one-head number is one. The means are comparable; the dispersions are not.

\subsection{Other operations}
\label{sec:ops}

Everything above is $(a+b) \bmod 97$. Two other binary operations on the same
vocabulary ask whether the delay is about addition or about the group the
network has to discover. $(a-b) \bmod 97$ is the \emph{same} group,
$\mathbb{Z}/97$ --- negating the second operand relabels the answer, so the
circuit of Section~\ref{sec:attribution} transfers unchanged.
$(a \times b) \bmod 97$ is the interesting one: on the nonzero residues it is
the cyclic multiplicative group of order $p - 1 = 96$, and the discrete
logarithm to a primitive root makes it isomorphic to addition mod 96, with the
$2p - 1 = 193$ pairs involving a zero sitting outside the group as a constant
the network can only memorize.

All three grok at strong weight decay in every seed, each memorizing at step
100 first, so the delay is not specific to addition. Subtraction is decisively
the slowest and the seeds agree: at $wd = 1$ its range does not overlap
addition's or multiplication's at all. At $wd = 0.1$ the difference becomes
qualitative --- two of three seeds never grok inside the budget (final test
accuracy $0.148$ and $0.047$) and the third only makes it at step 24{,}800.
Multiplication, meanwhile, is \emph{not} measurably faster than addition;
that correction matters because seed 0 alone showed 1{,}000 against 1{,}900 and
invited a story about the highly composite group order $96 = 2^5 \cdot 3$
offering more low-order frequencies. With three seeds the medians are 1{,}100
against 1{,}300 with almost entirely overlapping ranges. The isomorphism
argument survives as an explanation of why multiplication groks at all; it does
not survive as an explanation of it grokking sooner.

So the group is not the operative variable: subtraction has the identical group
to addition and is reliably the hardest of the three. The one structural
property that distinguishes it is that $a - b$ is the only non-commutative
operation here --- and Section~\ref{sec:attention} finds that grokking
symmetrizes how the ``='' position reads the two operands.

\paragraph{Testing that, which needs the right statistic.} Demanding
$L(a,b) = L(b,a)$ of a subtraction model is demanding that it be wrong, so a
defect there would show nothing. What $a - b = -(b-a)$ licenses instead is
\emph{anti}-equivariance: swapping the operands should permute the logit
vector by $c \mapsto -c \bmod p$. Computing both statistics for both
operations, each normalized by the logits' own standard deviation and read
against a shuffle baseline of $1.11$ (the level a defect reads when there is no
symmetry to find), gives Figure~\ref{fig:swap}: addition's invariance defect
collapses $0.560 \to 0.010$ while the symmetry it does not have stays pinned
at the no-symmetry level, and subtraction goes the other way in both columns
--- its invariance defect \emph{rises} to $1.086$, essentially the baseline,
while its anti-equivariance defect falls $1.034 \to 0.271$.

Grokking does not buy commutativity. It buys whichever swap symmetry the
operation actually has. Two things that do not fit that clean statement belong
with it rather than in a footnote. Subtraction acquires its symmetry far less
completely --- $0.271$ against addition's $0.010$ is $27\times$, and its seed
spread ($0.15$--$0.51$) says it is not a stable quantity, which is at least
consistent with subtraction being the slowest of the three though it does not
show that it is why. And at $wd = 0.1$ only one subtraction seed generalized at
all, so that cell is a single run: its anti-equivariance defect is $0.553$
while the two failed seeds sit at $0.86$ and $0.97$, near the no-symmetry
level, which is what a run that never found the structure should look like.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Nothing in this paper is a discovery. This section attributes each result it
reproduces and is deliberately specific about which parts of the account above
are somebody else's, because a reimplementation write-up is exactly the kind of
document in which that can blur.

\citet{power2022grokking} reported grokking and established the setting used
here --- small algorithmic datasets, modular arithmetic, a train/test split of
an exhaustive table, and the observation that generalization can arrive long
after the training loss has flattened. The phenomenon this paper measures is
theirs.

\citet{nanda2023progress} opened the trained network on modular addition and
identified the angle-addition circuit: embeddings that place each residue on a
circle at a handful of frequencies, and logits assembled by interference so
that they depend on $a + b$. They introduced the restricted and excluded losses
as progress measures showing the circuit forming during the delay.
Section~\ref{sec:fourier}, Section~\ref{sec:attribution} and
Section~\ref{sec:progress} reproduce those measurements, and
Section~\ref{sec:progress} follows their construction directly. Where this
paper differs from them it differs in setup rather than in substance --- a
different modulus, model size and optimizer put the pre-transition accuracy at
a different height --- and Section~\ref{sec:progress} says so at the point
where the difference shows.

\citet{liu2023omnigrok} made the parameter norm central, arguing that grokking
is what happens when a network initialized at a large norm has to travel to a
small-norm generalizing solution. Section~\ref{sec:wd} and
Section~\ref{sec:fourier} reproduce the norm trajectory that account predicts.
Two results here qualify it rather than confirm it: the dropout control
(Section~\ref{sec:controls-arms}) groks with a norm that grows monotonically,
so norm reduction is sufficient and not necessary in this setting, and the
scope ablation (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) finds that the decay has to act on the
readout circuit, so the operative quantity is not $\lVert\theta\rVert$ over the
whole network.

\citet{varma2023efficiency} explain the delay in terms of circuit efficiency:
the memorizing and generalizing circuits are both available, memorizing is
cheaper per unit of training loss when the training set is small, and a
regularizer that penalizes parameter mass eventually makes the generalizing
circuit the better deal. That is the account Section~\ref{sec:frac}'s mechanism
paragraph states, and Section~\ref{sec:surface}'s result --- more data
substitutes for weight decay, multiplicatively --- is what it predicts, in the
weak sense that a consistent measurement is not evidence for a mechanism over
its alternatives. Nothing here tests that account against a competitor.

What this paper contributes over those four is stated in
Section~\ref{sec:intro} and is entirely methodological: controls that separate
explanations which the single-configuration presentation leaves entangled, and
seed spread on read-outs that are normally reported from one checkpoint. The
implementation is written from scratch, which is why the read-outs exist, but
an independent reimplementation agreeing with published results is a weaker
kind of evidence than it feels like from the inside --- it agrees with them
because they are correct, and the interesting cases would be the ones where it
did not.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{One task.} Every result here is a binary operation on residues mod
97 (mod 113 once), on a 223k-parameter one-layer transformer, at one width and
depth. Section~\ref{sec:ops} varies the operation and Section~\ref{sec:heads}
varies the head count at fixed $d_{\text{model}}$; nothing varies the depth,
the width, the architecture family or the data domain. Grokking outside
algorithmic data \citep{liu2023omnigrok} is not touched.

\paragraph{The budget is a variable, and it is doing work.} Runs stop at
25{,}000 steps. Three cells of the Section~\ref{sec:surface} grid never grok
inside that, and the resulting ``cliff'' at $wd = 0$ is the edge of the
compute rather than a feature of the dynamics --- Section~\ref{sec:surface}
shows the censored cells are consistent with the same multiplicative law as
the rest. Nothing here says what those cells would do at $10^5$ steps.

\paragraph{Three seeds on the grid, five elsewhere, one on the controls.}
The exact test's two-sided floor is $0.10$ at three versus three, so the
grid ranks cells and compares steps against spreads but certifies no pairwise
comparison; the five-seed arms reach $0.008$ and can reach nothing smaller, so
``complete separation'' is the strongest statement available and finer
distinctions are simply out of reach at this sample size. The four control arms
of Section~\ref{sec:controls-arms} and Section~\ref{sec:scope} are one seed
each and are stated as directions.

\paragraph{Two checkpoints, not a trajectory.} The mechanistic read-outs of
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} are measured at the memorization point and at the
end. Only the progress measures of Section~\ref{sec:progress} and the attention
read-outs of Section~\ref{sec:attention} are instrumented per evaluation, and
both are single runs. Claims about \emph{when} something forms therefore rest
on one seed, which is why Section~\ref{sec:progress} quantifies the pre-jump
share of a read-out's rise rather than asserting that it rises early.

\paragraph{The run-length confound is reported, not removed.} Seed~0 trained
11{,}100 steps and seeds 1--4 stopped at 1{,}800--2{,}100
(Section~\ref{sec:setup}), so ``the state of the circuit after grokking''
means two different amounts of post-transition weight decay depending on the
seed. Section~\ref{sec:seeds} shows the conclusions hold on the four matched
runs alone, at the weaker floor four versus four permits. What remains unknown
is how much of the measured sparsification is the transition and how much is
the 9{,}000 steps after it; separating those needs the short runs retrained to
a matched budget, which has not been done.

\paragraph{Reproduction, not replication.} The implementation is independent
but the experimental design is not: it follows \citet{nanda2023progress}'s
choices closely enough that a systematic error in those choices would be
reproduced here rather than caught.
